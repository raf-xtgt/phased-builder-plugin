# Ideation Guide: Agent Reference

Ideation goes wide before it goes narrow. Your job: draw out the user's thinking, add alternatives they
didn't consider, bring real research, and help them decide. Don't write their product for them.

## Brainstorming moves
- **Brain dump first.** Let them talk without structure. Speech-to-text helps.
- **Restate the problem, not the solution.** "So the pain is X for Y. Your idea solves it by Z." Ideas get better
  when the problem is clear.
- **Generate alternatives (2–4).** Vary one thing at a time: a different user, a different format (app, browser
  extension, CLI, bot, physical object), a narrower job, a bolder version, a no-code version. Weight them equally;
  don't push your favorite.
- **Steal from analogies.** "How do other fields solve this?" (a sandglass app might borrow from meditation apps,
  game feedback loops, physical desk toys).
- **Find the kernel.** "If you removed everything except one thing, what must stay?"
- **Kill weak ideas kindly but clearly.** If research shows a direction is crowded, infeasible, or solves no real
  pain, say so with the evidence.

## Research (search the web, cite every claim with link and date)
- **Competitors:** search app stores, Product Hunt, GitHub, and the general web for products solving the same
  pain. For each: what it does, price, what users praise and complain about (reviews are good evidence of gaps).
  Aim for 3–6 real competitors; say if there are none and what that might mean.
- **Market signals:** forums, communities, and search interest showing people want this. Treat numbers from
  marketing pages with caution; prefer primary sources.
- **Feasibility:** check that key APIs, libraries or platform features exist and are maintained; check their
  limits, prices and region availability. A quick "is this possible at all?" check, not a design.
- **What's being built right now:** recent launches and open-source projects in the same space.
- Never invent a competitor, a price, or a number. If you can't verify something, say so.

## Opportunity alignment (only when a brief exists)
Read the brief: judging criteria, tracks, required tech and its depth check. For each direction, say how well it
fits and whether the required tech would be **deep** (essential) or **shallow** (bolted on). Ask: "Would you build
this without the opportunity?" Record the answer. If the best opportunity fit is a weak product, say so plainly.

## Converging
When directions are clear and researched, help them choose with a short comparison: problem strength, gap in the
market, feasibility, fit with their goals (and the opportunity, if any). Use a decision card if the choice is close.
The user decides.

## Exchange budget
Usually 5–8 meaningful exchanges, research in between. Draft `ideation.md` as soon as there's something to show and
update it as you go. Don't stretch the brainstorm once a decision is clear.
