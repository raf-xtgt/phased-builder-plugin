# Opportunity Research Rules: Agent Reference

These apply to every opportunity type. Read this first, then the type's own reference.

## 1. Search first, always
Never answer from memory about programs, deadlines, prizes, rules, eligibility, terms, or platform
policies. Search the web, open the official page, and read it. Search results alone are not enough.

## 2. Confirm it is open right now
- Find the current edition or cohort and its dates. Programs repeat; last year's page is a trap.
- If it's closed, expired, paused, or the page is out of date, say so plainly at the top.
- Rolling applications: say "rolling" and note when the next review or cohort starts, if stated.

## 3. Dates and time zones
Write every deadline with its official time zone and converted to the user's time zone. Add the days left.
If the time zone is not stated, say so and use the safest reading (the earliest possible moment).

## 4. Eligibility before anything else
Check and put any blocker at the very top of the brief:
- Country of residence and citizenship; countries excluded (sanctions, legal limits, "US only").
- Company registration needed? Where (e.g. a specific country or state)?
- In-person attendance, relocation, or travel needed? Visa implications?
- Age, student status, team size, solo founders allowed, full-time commitment.
- Payment and payout: can the user actually receive prize money or funding in their country?
  (Payout platforms and banks differ by country.)
- Stage and sector limits.
If something can't be confirmed, mark it **unverified** and say how to confirm (FAQ, email the organizers).

## 5. Check legitimacy
Signs to flag: application or "processing" fees that aren't standard, pressure to pay or decide fast,
no verifiable organizers or past participants, terms that take IP or equity unexpectedly, prizes that
can't be traced to a real sponsor. Say what you found; don't accuse.

## 6. Sources
Prefer the official page, rules/terms PDF, FAQ, and the organizer's own announcements. Record every source
in `research.md` with its URL and the date you checked it. Never invent a source or a figure. Quote at most
a short phrase; summarize the rest in your own words.

## 7. Terms are not advice
Point out terms that matter (equity taken, IP assignment, exclusivity, payout conditions) in plain words,
and say this isn't legal or investment advice; the user should read the terms themselves, and for money or
equity, ask a qualified person.

## 8. Fit and gap
Compare what the opportunity needs with what exists today (`PRODUCT.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`), or with the
idea if nothing is built yet. List gaps. For each gap, propose the smallest change, sized as one or more
features (`3-scope`), design changes (`4-architecture`), or setup (`5-setup`). The user decides.

## 9. Writing
You may draft text (applications, pitch, posts) from an interview with the user, using their words and
real facts from the build log. Mark every draft as a draft. Never invent traction, users, revenue,
partnerships or credentials. The user reviews, edits, and submits or posts it themselves. If the user wants
to write it all themselves, help only with questions, gaps, spelling and grammar.
